When Rain Becomes Risk

This visualisation explores Malaysia’s precipitation and temperature patterns before connecting heavy rainfall to natural disaster risk. The story begins with where and when rainfall is highest, then moves into rainfall abnormality and flood-related disasters.

This project explores a relationship, not direct causation. Flood impacts are affected by rainfall, drainage, land use, urbanisation, river systems, and population exposure.

1. Malaysia’s rainfall changes by place and month

Rainfall is not evenly distributed across Malaysia. Some states receive more rain, and the wettest months also differ across regions. The map below allows the viewer to compare rainfall by month.

Use the dropdown to view average rainfall for each month. Darker states indicate higher rainfall.

The heatmap gives a compact view of rainfall patterns across states and months.

2. State-level rainfall differences

The following maps summarise recent rainfall by state. They show average rainfall, normal rainfall, difference from normal rainfall, rainfall ratio, and a simple rainfall category.

3. Which states are wetter or above normal?

Maps are useful for location, but ranking charts make comparison easier. The bar chart ranks rainfall by state, while the lollipop chart highlights how far each state is above or below normal.

Points above the expected relationship suggest states where recent rainfall is higher than normal.

4. Rainfall and natural disaster risk

Heavy rainfall is important because flooding is one of the most relevant disaster risks in Malaysia. The charts below show the disaster profile and how flood records change over time.
