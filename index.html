When Rain Becomes Risk

This visualisation explores Malaysia’s rainfall and temperature patterns before connecting precipitation to natural disaster risk. The story moves from climate patterns to floods, showing why rainfall matters for disaster preparedness.

This project explores relationships, not direct causation. Flood impacts are also affected by drainage, land use, urbanisation, river systems, and population exposure.

1. Rainfall patterns across months and states

Malaysia receives rainfall throughout the year, but the intensity differs by month and by state. These charts show when rainfall increases and which states record higher average rainfall.

2. Temperature context

Temperature provides climate context for the rainfall story. Malaysia’s temperatures are relatively stable, but monthly minimum, average, and maximum values still show seasonal variation.

3. Natural disasters and flood risk

Heavy rainfall matters because floods are the most common disaster type in the dataset. The following charts show Malaysia’s disaster profile and how flood records change over time.
